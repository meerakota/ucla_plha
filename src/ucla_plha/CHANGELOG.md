Unreleased
* pygmm ground motion models with a logic tree of the ground motion distribution (the USGS epistemic branches of the median of the nshmp-lib NGA-West2 models, mu -/+ epsilon with weights 0.185, 0.63, 0.185, and of AG20, KBCG20, and PSBAH20) are now integrated branch by branch: the hazard, liquefaction hazard, and disaggregation sum the weighted exceedance probabilities of the branches, each truncated and with epsilon relative to its own mean, as nshmp-lib does (ExceedanceModel.treeExceedanceCombined, Disaggregator), instead of using the collapsed median ln(sum w exp(mu)), whose distribution has a much thinner upper tail. This raises the NSHM23 WUS fault hazard at high ground motions (rate ratio to the USGS NSHM at 1 g from 0.90 to 1.02 in the Bay Area and 1.01 in Seattle) and the Cascadia interface and slab hazard (e.g. 0.66 to 1.01 at 1 g for the interface at Seattle), and lowers the disaggregation epsilon (Hayward north at the Bay Area, 2475 years: 1.53 to 1.32; USGS 1.30). Requires pygmm with GroundMotionModel.ln_branches; older pygmm versions give one (collapsed) branch.
* Stable crust sites on the Gulf and Atlantic coastal plain now get the location-dependent site data of the USGS NSHM, as nshmp-lib gives them to every site (model.SiteData, calc.Sites; also the sites of the USGS hazard web service, which take only longitude, latitude, and Vs30): the coastal plain sediment thickness zsed (Boyd 2023, nshm-conus 6.2.0 site-data/margin) unless "site" gives "zsed", and, inside the NSHM "Coastal Plain CPA region" (site-data/gmm-region), its stable crust ground motion model tree (NGA_EAST_2026 1/6, NGA_EAST_2026_CPA 1/6, NGA_EAST_2026_ADJUSTED 1/3, and the same for the seed models) for all stable crust source models that use the default tree, including nshm23_ceus_grid_system. The Chapman and Guo (2021) coastal plain amplification lowers PGA by about 60% at zSed of about 1 km. This removes the high bias of the CEUS hazard at New Madrid (2475-year PGA of the Fault component +29% to +10%, Grid +5% to -5%; FaultCluster +8% to -1%) and Memphis (Fault +30% to +12%, Grid +10% to -3%); the remaining differences were from the NGA-East epistemic branches, which pygmm collapsed; with a pygmm whose NgaEast and NgaEastSeeds give ln_branches (17 median models or 14 seeds, times the EPRI and panel standard deviations, as nshmp-lib), the branch-by-branch integration reproduces the USGS NSHM CEUS hazard at New Madrid, Memphis, Charleston, Central Virginia, and a site in Nebraska (2475-year PGA within 1% for every component, apart from the small Fault component in Nebraska, +5%; rate ratios 1.00 at 0.01 to 1 g at New Madrid and Memphis). "site": {"nshm_site_data": false} turns the site data off. The data are in site_data/coastal_plain.npz (utilities/convert_nshm23_site_data.py).
* Cluster source models: all ruptures of a cluster are used if any of its ruptures is within the distance cutoff, as in nshmp-lib (ClusterRuptureSet.checkDistance), instead of filtering the ruptures one by one (New Madrid cluster at Central Virginia, about 1000 km: rate ratio to the USGS NSHM at 0.01 g from 0.36 to 1.02).
* Rx and Rx1 of fault ruptures with several fault segments are now those of the segment with the smallest Rrup, as in nshmp-lib (SystemRuptureSet), instead of the minimum over the segments, which, because Rx is signed, was the most footwall-side segment and often turned hanging-wall sites into footwall sites. The earlier convention is available with "constraints": {"rupture_rx": "minimum"}.
* ucerf3_fm31 and ucerf3_fm32 fault geometry and rupture depths now follow UCERF3 as implemented in OpenSHA and the USGS NSHM. (1) Aseismicity: the conversion used the full subsection planes from the trace to the lower depth, although UCERF3 reduces each subsection's area by its aseismic slip factor (0.1 for most faults, up to 0.9 for creeping faults) and its rupture areas and magnitudes are those of the reduced planes (OpenSHA FaultSection.getReducedAveUpperDepth, StirlingGriddedSurface; FaultSystemRupSet.getSurfaceForRupture with aseisReducesArea, the BaseFaultSystemSolutionERF default; nshmp-lib and the nshm-conus UCERF3 ruptures.csv). The upper edge of each subsection now moves down-dip from the trace to upper depth + aseismic slip factor * (lower depth - upper depth) and the lower edge does not move: dipping subsections lose up to 4.9 km (San Pedro Escarpment) of their up-dip part, and the rRup of controlling dipping subsections increases by up to about 1.5 km and agrees with the USGS 2018 NSHM disaggregation (e.g. Ventura - Pitas Point 1.07 to 2.63 km, USGS 2.65; Elysian Park 3.84 to 5.38 km, USGS 5.40). (2) Rupture ztor and zbor are now the averages of the reduced subsection upper and lower depths weighted by the reduced subsection areas, as OpenSHA (CompoundSurface.getAveRupTopDepth, getAveRupBottomDepth) and the nshm-conus ruptures.csv (and the nshm23_wus model), instead of the shallowest upper depth and deepest lower depth; the rupture dip is weighted by the reduced areas. Compared with the USGS 2018 NSHM FaultSystem hazard (UCERF3, PGA, Vs30 760 m/s, nshmp ground motion models, rates scaled by the 0.97 of the nshm-conus ruptures.csv), the 475- and 2475-year PGA differences go from -4.4%/-3.0% to -0.3%/+0.0% at Moss Landing, +0.4%/+2.8% to +0.3%/+1.1% in Los Angeles, -2.4%/-1.5% to -0.2%/-0.1% in the Bay Area, and +1.1%/+3.0% to +0.1%/+1.0% in Ventura. The example configurations (Moss Landing) change by -0.04% (BSSA14 only) to +4.3% in 475- and 2475-year PGA (+1.4% to +2.0% for the four NGA-West2 models) and -1.6% to -2.5% in FSL. utilities/convert_ucerf3_fault_source_models.py reproduces the earlier files with UCERF3_APPLY_ASEISMICITY=0 UCERF3_RUPTURE_DEPTHS=shallowest.
* nshm23_wus fault geometry: the aseismic reduction of the seismogenic width now removes the top of the dipping fault plane (the upper edge moves down-dip, as in nshmp-lib DefaultGriddedSurface) instead of moving the whole plane down, which displaced dipping faults toward their footwall by aseismicity * (lower depth - upper depth) / tan(dip) (2.5 to 2.9 km for the Puente Hills and Compton thrusts).
