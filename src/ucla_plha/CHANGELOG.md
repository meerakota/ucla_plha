Unreleased
* Stable crust sites on the Gulf and Atlantic coastal plain now get the location-dependent site data of the USGS NSHM, as nshmp-lib gives them to every site (model.SiteData, calc.Sites; also the sites of the USGS hazard web service, which take only longitude, latitude, and Vs30): the coastal plain sediment thickness zsed (Boyd 2023, nshm-conus 6.2.0 site-data/margin) unless "site" gives "zsed", and, inside the NSHM "Coastal Plain CPA region" (site-data/gmm-region), its stable crust ground motion model tree (NGA_EAST_2026 1/6, NGA_EAST_2026_CPA 1/6, NGA_EAST_2026_ADJUSTED 1/3, and the same for the seed models) for all stable crust source models that use the default tree, including nshm23_ceus_grid_system. The Chapman and Guo (2021) coastal plain amplification lowers PGA by about 60% at zSed of about 1 km. This removes the high bias of the CEUS hazard at New Madrid (2475-year PGA of the Fault component +29% to +10%, Grid +5% to -5%; FaultCluster +8% to -1%) and Memphis (Fault +30% to +12%, Grid +10% to -3%); the remaining differences are from the NGA-East epistemic branches. "site": {"nshm_site_data": false} turns the site data off. The data are in site_data/coastal_plain.npz (utilities/convert_nshm23_site_data.py).
* Cluster source models: all ruptures of a cluster are used if any of its ruptures is within the distance cutoff, as in nshmp-lib (ClusterRuptureSet.checkDistance), instead of filtering the ruptures one by one (New Madrid cluster at Central Virginia, about 1000 km: rate ratio to the USGS NSHM at 0.01 g from 0.36 to 1.02).
* Rx and Rx1 of fault ruptures with several fault segments are now those of the segment with the smallest Rrup, as in nshmp-lib (SystemRuptureSet), instead of the minimum over the segments, which, because Rx is signed, was the most footwall-side segment and often turned hanging-wall sites into footwall sites. The earlier convention is available with "constraints": {"rupture_rx": "minimum"}.
* nshm23_wus fault geometry: the aseismic reduction of the seismogenic width now removes the top of the dipping fault plane (the upper edge moves down-dip, as in nshmp-lib DefaultGriddedSurface) instead of moving the whole plane down, which displaced dipping faults toward their footwall by aseismicity * (lower depth - upper depth) / tan(dip) (2.5 to 2.9 km for the Puente Hills and Compton thrusts).
